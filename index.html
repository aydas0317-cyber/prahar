INDIAN ARMY — COMMAND DECISION TRAINING SYSTEM

Trains commanders to make time-critical decisions under incomplete, delayed, and conflicting information

Existing training formats (TEWT, CPX) assume complete and reliable information flow. PRAHAR deliberately injects incomplete, delayed, or contradictory intelligence feeds — training decision-making under uncertainty rather than under ideal conditions.

SYSTEM OVERVIEW

How the System Works

01

Instructor Control

The instructor initiates the scenario and injects communication failures (delay, blackout, packet loss) and contradictory intelligence — all in real time.

→
02

Degraded Information

The commander receives incomplete, delayed, or contradictory information. Each source has a distinct reliability score. Decisions must be made without complete data.

→
03

Decision + Rationale

The commander selects a course of action, sets a confidence level, and submits a written rationale. The system records everything — what was decided, why, how confident, and how long it took.

→
04

Automated AAR

After the exercise, the system generates an After-Action Review: decision timeline, information snapshot, confidence trend, comms failures, cascade detection, AI evaluation, and exportable report.

This system does not simply test whether the final decision was correct. It evaluates the decision process — how the commander handled conflicting information, weighted source reliability, managed time pressure, and tracked cascading effects.

INNOVATION

Core Innovations

01

Trust-Weighted Information Scoring

Every intelligence source carries a dynamic reliability score. Trainees must learn to weight conflicting sources against each other under time pressure.

02

Asymmetric Information Per Role

Each role receives different data. The commander sees the full picture, the Cyber/EW officer sees spectrum data, the Intel analyst sees the source reliability matrix.

03

Decision Confidence Slider

Trainees self-report their confidence with each decision. The system tracks how confidence aligns with information quality — detecting overconfidence and hesitation.

04

What Did You Know at the Time?

Each decision is evaluated against the information available at that exact moment — not with hindsight. A snapshot of the intel feed is captured with every submission.

05

Cascade Detection

Based on research showing that one erroneous decision can trigger a self-reinforcing cycle of cascading failures. The system tracks and visualizes these cascades across the command structure.

Initial error at T+02:00
Field requests +3 at T+02:15
Overload threshold exceeded — cascade detected
06

Explainable AAR

The AI does not simply produce a score. It explains why the score was assigned — which sources were trusted, which were ignored, and how uncertainty was handled. Exportable as HTML and JSON.

RESEARCH BACKING

Scientific Foundation

PAPER 1

Self-Reinforcing Degradation in Decision-making Teams

A single erroneous decision can trigger a self-reinforcing cycle of cascading failures across the entire command structure. The S-curve model demonstrates that decision accuracy decreases non-linearly as workload increases.

Hubbard, Kott, Martin
arXiv:1607.08139 →
PAPER 2

WARBENCH: Benchmark for LLMs in Military Decision-Making

Nine AI models tested across 136 real-world conflict scenarios. At 40% information loss, even the most advanced models exhibit catastrophic performance degradation. Small models violated international law in 70% of cases.

Li, Wang, Xie, Ma, Wang
arXiv:2603.21280 →
